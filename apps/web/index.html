01 / THE PROBLEMPUBLIC LEDGERS, PRIVATE LIVES

Your salary shouldn't become public data just because payroll moves on-chain.

Verification should answer a question. It should not open a person's entire compensation history.

PayDrip separates what must be proven from what should remain private.

03 / HOW PAYDRIP WORKSFOUR INTENTIONAL STEPS

Private by default.
Verifiable when needed.

The protocol starts with an issuer-authorized record. A proof only makes sense when it stays bound to that record.

01

Create

An administrator opens a payroll epoch and authorizes a randomized commitment to a record.

↗
02

Keep private

Salary, employee secret, and commitment randomness remain private inputs; the ledger keeps the commitment and lifecycle state.

◐
03

Prove

The employee uses the record opening and their secret to make an employment or fixed income-tier claim.

↗

A verifier checks the finalized claim in its request context without receiving the exact salary.

06 / PRIVACY MODELA PRECISE BOUNDARY

Privacy lives in
the protocol.

The ledger keeps the state needed to verify a claim. Sensitive record openings remain private inputs.

↗WHAT CAN BE PUBLIC
  • Organization and deployment domain IDs
  • Payroll epoch and lifecycle status
  • Record commitment and revocation handles
  • Claim context, tier, and linked record handle

Public handles and transactions can reveal timing and relationships.

◐WHAT STAYS PRIVATE
  • Exact monthly base salary
  • Employee secret and record opening
  • Commitment randomness
  • Other values inside the private record

The issuer knows what it records. PayDrip does not hide compensation from that issuer.
